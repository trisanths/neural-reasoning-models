# Retrieval training on real documents

Every retrieval episode this model had trained on was invented here: our
systems, our generated frame grammar, our task families, our page format. It
had never issued a query against text somebody else wrote and never seen a
question a person asked. This lane builds retrieval training from real
questions against real passages, trains on it, and re-measures.

Numbers are generated by `src/realret/report.py` into `src/realret/tables.md`
from the record files it names, and its record-file table carries each file's
modification time so a stale quote shows up as one. Artifacts are archived at
`s3://decoupled-reasoner-009398924577/runs/real-v1-8k/`.

## The result in five lines

| question | before, corpus-v1-8k | after, real-v1-8k |
| --- | --- | --- |
| does it ask at all, MMLU with live web | 65 of 200 | 142 of 200 |
| does it ask at all, held-out real documents, greedy | 0.947 / 0.214 / 0.947 | 1.000 / 1.000 / 1.000 |
| can it answer from a real page, greedy | 0.004 / 0.003 / 0.020 | 0.278 / 0.279 / 0.417 |
| MMLU closed book, n=500, floor 0.250 | 0.2640 | 0.2820 |
| synthetic `chain_rule`, forced choice, floor 0.250 | 0.2520 | 0.2441 |

Triples are HotpotQA, Natural Questions, TriviaQA in that order.

## The measurement that leads

The failure this addresses is a policy failure, not a comprehension failure.
Shown a real question, the corpus-trained checkpoint often does not emit a
retrieve token at all, and when it does the query is a fragment copied out of
the prompt. Retrieval underneath is healthy: pages come back and they reach
the context.

On MMLU with the live web tier behind it, n=200 seed 1234, the corpus-trained
checkpoint emitted a retrieve token on 65 of 200 rollouts, 0.325. After
training on real documents it emits one on 142 of 200, 0.710. Pages entered
the context on exactly those rollouts in both cases, 0.325 and 0.710, so
nothing is lost between asking and reading.

The queries change as much as the rate. Before, they are prompt fragments:
`A. That law is essentially an artefact or social construction. What does it
claim?`, ` Yuk`, `enital absence of the vas deferens`. After, they are
question-derived: `herpes simplex virus`, `language largest number "speakers"
English`, `Marcia Baron argues artificiality ticking bomb cases problematic`.

On held-out real-document episodes the same measurement moves from 0.947,
0.214 and 0.947 to 1.000 on all three sources, and `no_query` disappears from
the failure decomposition: 0 of 410, 0 of 432 and 0 of 350 remaining errors.
Natural Questions is where the old behaviour was worst and where the move is
largest, which fits, because its questions are lowercase spoken-language
queries and look least like anything the synthetic corpus contained.

## MMLU does not move

Closed book, five-shot completion, n=500, seed 1234, floor 0.250, Wilson 95%:

| model | acc | interval |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| ours corpus-v1-8k | 0.2640 | 0.2273 to 0.3043 |
| ours corpus-v1-8k, eot prefix | 0.2620 | 0.2254 to 0.3023 |
| ours real-v1-8k | 0.2820 | 0.2443 to 0.3230 |
| ours real-v1-8k, eot prefix | 0.2880 | 0.2500 to 0.3292 |
| LFM2-350M, bos | 0.4360 | 0.3932 to 0.4798 |
| LFM2-350M, no bos | 0.3140 | 0.2749 to 0.3560 |

Every one of our intervals contains chance. LFM2-350M reproduces its
published 43.43 at 0.4360, which is the harness check for this whole
battery, and the 12.2 points between its bos and no-bos rows are the harness
gap this project already found, reproduced at the larger n.

With the same live web pages in context, n=500, every page served from the
cache the LFM2 run filled, so both sides are given identical pages by
construction and the run counter confirms it at zero live searches:

| model | closed book | with the pages |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| ours corpus-v1-8k | 0.2640 | 0.2600 matched format, 0.2340 native format |
| ours real-v1-8k | 0.2820 | 0.2700 matched format, 0.2620 native format |
| LFM2-350M | 0.4360 | 0.4780 |

That is the four-cell table and it is the sharpest result here. Given the
same pages, the matched-size general model converts them into accuracy and
ours does not: LFM2-350M gains 4.2 points, and on the 57 items whose pages
carry the gold answer string it scores 0.6316 against its own 0.4360 closed
book, while ours scores 0.281 there against 0.298 closed book.

Pages do not help either of our checkpoints. Four of 500 prompts exceeded the 4096
context and were cropped, mean context 2194 tokens, so this is not an
overflow artefact; the uncropped subset gives the same number.

Retrieval training taught this model to ask and to read. It did not give it
the knowledge MMLU tests, and it did not teach it to pick a letter: the
generation cell shows the model naming one of A to D on 0.045 of rollouts
even after training, up from 0.02.

## The named surviving failure did not move

`src/corpus/RETRAIN.md` records `chain_rule` at 0.220 against a 0.250 floor
and `weighted_chain` at 0.039 as the cells the diversity corpus could not
move. Measured here on the held-out band, both checkpoints, same items:

| cell | instrument | n | floor | before | after |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| chain_rule | forced choice | 1536 | 0.250 | 0.2520 | 0.2441 |
| chain_rule | generation, env grader | 384 | 0.000 | 0.4922 | 0.4609 |
| weighted_chain | generation, env grader | 1536 | 0.000 | 0.2051 | 0.1849 |

`weighted_chain` carries no candidates, so it has a generation cell only,
which is how `RETRAIN.md` reports it too.

Chance-corrected, `chain_rule` forced choice goes from 0.003 to -0.008: at the
floor before and at the floor after. So learning to chain two hops over real
Wikipedia paragraphs, which the HotpotQA numbers show did happen, does not
transfer to chaining over an invented relation system. Whatever `chain_rule`
requires, it is not the retrieval habit and not multi-hop practice on real
text. That is a more specific statement of the failure than the project had
before.

## What is still broken, precisely

With `no_query` at zero, every remaining error on held-out real documents is
one of two things, and they are about half each. On HotpotQA greedy, of 410
wrong answers, 178 are `bad_pages`, meaning the model asked and the retriever
returned nothing carrying the answer, and 232 are `did_not_read`, meaning a
served page carried the gold answer and the answer was still wrong. Natural
Questions splits 229 to 203 and TriviaQA 156 to 194. `truncated` is 0 in every
cell, so no error is a budget artefact.

Only `did_not_read` is a claim about the model. `bad_pages` is a retriever
quality result and it is the more tractable half.

## What was built

Three sources, all real, all with the passages the datasets ship.

| source | question | document | hops |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: |
| `hotpot_qa` | a HotpotQA distractor question | one of the ten titled Wikipedia paragraphs the dataset supplies, two of them supporting | 2 |
| `natural_questions` | a Google query, from MRQA's NaturalQuestionsShort | a passage of the Wikipedia page a human annotator marked | 1 |
| `trivia_qa` | a trivia question, from MRQA's TriviaQA-web | a passage of a web document a search engine returned | 1 |

Counts, from `src/realret/sources.py` and `src/realret/episodes.py`:

| --- | ---: | ---: | --- | --- |
| hotpot_qa train | 90,029 | 80,906 | 9,086 unplannable, 37 answer off page | 2: 68,207, 3: 12,699 |
| hotpot_qa validation | 7,377 | 6,733 | 639, 5 | 2: 5,604, 3: 1,129 |
| natural_questions train | 56,896 | 48,733 | 8,163 unplannable | 1: 23,566, 2: 25,167 |
| natural_questions validation | 6,897 | 6,160 | 737 | 1: 3,341, 2: 2,819 |

| trivia_qa validation | 7,752 | 6,790 | 962 | 1: 2,836, 2: 3,954 |

The HotpotQA train row reproduced exactly on two different machines, same
counts and same histograms, which is the strongest check available that the
build is deterministic in its inputs and seeds.

HotpotQA carries the largest share of the mixture because chaining a
retrieved result is the failure that survived every earlier data
intervention, and it is the only one of the three whose retrieval corpus is
multi-hop by construction: its eight distractors were retrieved by the
dataset's authors against the gold pair, so the bundle is a corpus the
dataset supplies rather than one assembled here.

MRQA ships one passage document per question, so the bundle for the two
single-hop sources is that document's own passages plus mined negatives:
passages belonging to other questions that share an uncommon term with this
one. A negative about the same entity is the only kind worth having, because
a bundle of unrelated pages makes any query look good.

## Train and eval split

Splits are the datasets' own train and validation files. Evaluation sets are
a seeded sample of 600 episodes per source out of the validation build, with
a 300-episode subset for the sampled decodes; a sample rather than the head
of the file, because the validation files arrive in dataset order.

After de-contamination, shared question hashes between train and eval are 0
of 600 on all three sources. Documents do overlap, 0.28, 0.28 and 0.05 of
evaluation document keys, which is normal for Wikipedia-derived corpora and
is not answer leakage, since the questions differ.

## Benchmark contamination

Checked against the MMLU, GSM8K, ARC-Challenge and WinoGrande test items in
the same parquet files `src/extern/bench.py` scores on: 16,630 stems of eight
words or more. Both scans cover every document of every bundle, not only the
ones a planned trace serves.

| | raw build | what was trained |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| training questions scanned | 182,556 | 182,515 |
| documents scanned | 2,027,405 | 2,026,913 |
| characters scanned | 920,490,119 | 920,287,184 |
| exact matches | 37 (mmlu 29, arc 8) | 0 |
| near matches at Jaccard 0.5 | 19 | 17 |

The 37 were real. MMLU items such as `how many books are in the new
testament` and `how many stars are on the american flag` appear verbatim as
TriviaQA and Natural Questions training questions or inside their pages.
`src/realret/decontam.py` removed 41 episodes of 182,556: 32 for a verbatim
benchmark stem, 1 for a near match at 0.7, and 8 TriviaQA questions whose
content hash also appeared in an evaluation file. HotpotQA lost none.

The 17 surviving near matches are different questions that share phrasing,
the closest being `what is the birthstone for the month of january` against
`what is the birthstone for the month of may`. The check only considers
stems of eight words or more, because a shorter stem cannot be matched
without generating false positives; short MMLU questions are outside what it
covers, and that is a limit of the check rather than a claim about them.

## The trace

The episode format is the one `src/rl/env.py` and `src/evals/interactive.py`
already walk, and the prompt comes from `src/rl/env.py:build_prompt`, so the
world header is present. Without it this checkpoint issues no retrieval
round, emits no answer marker and runs to the token cap.

    <|world|> domain: web <|q|> question
    [ <|retrieve|> query <|result|> page ] x rounds
    <|a|> answer <|eot|>

The domain string is `web` for all three sources, one header rather than
three, so the model has to decide from the question whether an item needs
chaining instead of reading that off the header. The source travels in the
pack index, which is what the per-source tables group by.

Round one always writes the question itself as the query, compressed to the
environment's 24 token cap by dropping stopwords first and then leading
words, always on a word boundary. This is the policy that has to transfer: a
live web search takes a question, not a term vector. Cutting on the raw token
tail, which is what the synthetic corpus renderer does, turns "Allie Goertz"
into "z"; harmless for a synthetic question with a padding lead-in and wrong
for a real one.

Later rounds refine. A refinement starts from the question's content words
and adds bridge terms drawn only from the question and from pages already
served, chosen greedily by BM25 margin and accepted the moment the serving
gate actually returns the target with earlier rounds excluded. Letting the
builder write any verifying query instead produced single tokens like "her"
and "to", and a policy trained on those carries them to a search box.

The no-clairvoyance rule is the one `src/scrub/web_retrieval.py` enforces, and
`src/realret/verify.py` re-checks every contract from the written episode
alone rather than from the object that built it. Clean rates on a 4000
episode stride sample: 0.9998, 1.0000 and 0.9995 on the three training files
and 1.0000 on all three evaluation files, the only failures being three
episodes whose rendered length exceeds 1024 tokens, which the packer drops.

One to one, real to synthetic. The synthetic half is the diversity corpus the

sake: frame generalisation and the plan-length ceiling both moved on that

gain that was not yet measured. The frames table above shows the choice paid.

## The budget

The project's standard, matched to `corpus-v1-8k` exactly: 8000 optimizer
steps, batch 32, AdamW at 2e-5 with betas 0.9/0.95, no weight decay, gradient
clip 1.0, 200 warmup steps, cosine decay to a tenth, bfloat16 autocast, one
L40S. 6171 seconds. Answer loss 2.67 to 0.20, query loss 1.87 to 0.12. The
training pass is `src/corpus/sft.py train` unchanged; only the pack it reads
is new.

Batch 32 is reached as micro-batch 16 with two accumulations rather than the
previous run's 8 with four. That is a memory arrangement on a card this lane
had to itself and not a change to the optimizer step.

Matching the budget is the point. The ladder trains well below compute
optimal and more steps would probably help, but running more here would
confound the data change with a compute change, and the data change is the
question.

## Deviations and gaps, stated

- `src/extern/retrieval_mmlu.py` builds its input ids on the CPU while the
  model is on the GPU, so the LFM2-350M with-pages cell cannot run with
  `--device cuda`. That module belongs to another lane and was not modified;
  the cell was run on CPU instead.
- The contamination check only considers benchmark stems of eight words or
  more, as described above.
- `chain_rule` and `weighted_chain` were measured on the held-out band of the
  diversity corpus, not on the exact item selection `RETRAIN.md` used, so the
  before column here is this lane's own measurement of the control checkpoint
  and not a quote of that document's number.
