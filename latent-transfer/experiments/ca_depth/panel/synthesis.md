# Verdict on the six ideas

## 1. Ranking

Raw judge sums (novelty / evidence / testability):

SFL 3/6/6 = 15. WRM 3/6/6 = 15. Stencil Tape 4/5/5 = 14. Snap-Tape 2/6/5 = 13. DRM 3/5/5 = 13. Airlock 3/4/4 = 11.

Adjusted ranking after merging and overrides:

1. Frame Machine (SFL, Snap-Tape and DRM merged; see section 2).
2. WRM, which is the Frame Machine plus one scheduling claim.
3. Stencil Tape.
4. Airlock.

Overrides and why.

Snap-Tape's novelty 2 against SFL's 3 and DRM's 3 is judge variance on one idea. The Snap-Tape judge docked it for Bansal et al. 2202.05826: a continuous recurrent net with recall, trained with a progressive loss at 30 iterations, solving 801x801 mazes at 20,000 iterations with no discretization anywhere. That refutation is correct. It also applies word for word to SFL's claim that continuous carry caps at 1.5x and to DRM's claim that the k~4 ceiling is a property of analog residual carry, and neither of those was docked for it. Apply the objection evenly and the three score the same, which is right, because they are the same idea.

Stencil Tape drops from third to third-from-last despite the highest novelty score in the set. Its 4 was awarded for the compile-versus-attend ablation, which is a measurement. The same judge's strongest objection is fatal to the idea's purpose: on every task the researcher owns, the compile target is symbolic (a 32-entry table, a reference implementation in Python), so an LM that emits the table plus an exact interpreter beats a learned executor with soft compiled weights at every k with zero per-cell error. The evidence judge adds that the only in-base support for compile-to-weights, the 0.0283 in-context anomaly, was measured in a model that reads and computes in one residual stream; giving the executor its own network removes that condition on its own, and Xu's 59.5M model executes an in-context program perfectly. An ablation score does not outrank a fatal objection.

One partial override that changes what the top idea must test rather than where it ranks. SFL's novelty judge says the matched discrete-versus-continuous comparison "has been run" in Altabaa 2510.14095. Altabaa's continuous arm carries its own state and is supervised through a readout; only the discrete arm is teacher-forced (their Appendix B). Carry type and training regime change together, which is the same confound SFL's other two judges found in SFL's own design. The matched version (ground-truth embedding plus noise fed back, per-step CE, identical data) has not been run, and Bansal's result says the continuous arm could win it. That cell is open.

Judges wrong on citation but right on conclusion, no rank change. Airlock's evidence judge predicts rule-in-context equals rule-in-weights from Xu, but Xu puts the language semantics in weights and only the program in context; the right citations are AutomataGPT (98.5% one-step on unseen 2D CA rules given in context) and Burtsev 2412.01417, and they support the same conclusion. WRM's two judges disagree on whether the chained 1dCA frame clears 0.90 at k=4 (one says expected, one says 0.95^4 = 0.81 fails); SFL's evidence judge confirmed from the full Beyond Memorization paper that feeding a decoded state back is the one variant never run, so neither judge has grounds and the test is real.

## 2. Are they the same idea?

Yes. All six.

The shared core: a program prefix (causal, KV cached once) followed by W discrete slots at hard positions, one symbol each; one forward pass of a 5 to 60M bidirectional block predicts every slot's next symbol in parallel; argmax, write in place, discard the hidden state; train on single-step teacher-forced cross-entropy from the reference implementation with no BPTT; fixed step budget so the loop is one CUDA graph. Each generator adds one decoration. SFL: a diff-supervised hold gate. Snap-Tape: a theory of why it works (refuted). DRM: address/value write heads and a fixed codec. Stencil Tape: the program as a hypernetwork delta on a per-cell MLP. Airlock: the program as compiled one-hot weights, the LM out of the loop, SYNTRA-style selection. WRM: parallel-outermost targets so the loop count is critical-path depth.

The judges found the core in print four times. Altabaa et al., 2510.14095 (Oct 2025): argmax and re-embed between iterations, teacher-forced across iterations, per-step supervision, random state corruption for repair, trained on 32-node graphs, near-perfect at 128. Rodionov and Prokhorenkova, ICML 2025: discrete states, teacher-forced transitions, perfect to 1600 nodes with a correctness proof, plus a published negative for discrete state without stepwise supervision (79/0 at n=64). Papailiopoulos, SUBLEQ transformer round 2 (March 2026): 4.9M parameters, a 33-cell integer frame, single-step training with 100x weight on changed cells, argmax per cell in place, 100% single-step on held-out states, 99.8% over 804 unseen programs to 91 steps, four hours on a laptop. Ito et al., 2608.31067 (Aug 2026): depth-1 parallel reduction with in-place writeback and structural halting, 280 to 4,416 parameters, 100% to depth 30. Beside them, Bansal for continuous carry at 600x and Xu 2604.25166 for program-in-context at 60x serially.

## 3. None of these is a breakthrough

Said plainly: none of the six is a breakthrough, for one shared reason. They attack the carry, meaning how state crosses the step boundary. The evidence base plus what the judges added says the carry is not the bottleneck. Keep state in positions, supervise every step, train from on-distribution states, and depth extrapolates 4x (Altabaa), 10x (SUBLEQ), 60x (Xu), 600x (Bansal), with discretization or without it. Every one of the six turns its depth claim into a^k for a per-step accuracy a, and none of them raises a.

And a is a k=1 quantity. On held-out systems the verified values are 0.95 (1dCA, rule inferred from an orbit, 4-layer d=128), about 0.985 (AutomataGPT, unseen rules in context), and 0.68 (the researcher's 350M on prose rules). At 0.95, k=16 gives 0.44. At 0.985, k=64 gives 0.38. The thousands-of-steps headline needs a above 0.9999, and the only place the evidence has that number is a fixed language trained into the weights: Xu at roughly 10^-5 per token, SUBLEQ at roughly 10^-5 per step. So the depth problem for execution-shaped work is solved in print for known languages and reduces to the k=1 problem for unknown ones.

Two shared framing errors. All six cite the in-context anomaly (0.0283 at well-formedness 0.34 versus 0.1233 at 0.97) as the reason to separate program from state. That anomaly was measured in an autoregressive LM, whose between-token state is already discrete and closed under the step. Whatever caused it happened inside one forward pass, while the model was reading the textbook. No carry design touches it. And all six use the Qwen3.5-4B versus 27B split as the belief to change; LiveCodeBench, OSWorld, BabyVision and ZEROBench are search, tool use and perception, not execution of a stated procedure. A perfect executor does not move them.

A third point, from the Snap-Tape novelty judge, settles the framing. A "held-out rule system" is a program in a fixed language. A radius-2 CA rule is 32 bits of program for the semantics "look up"; a rewrite rule set is a program for "match and replace"; the invented formal systems ship with Python reference implementations. An executor for one universal language with the program in context therefore covers every system the generators can produce, and that regime belongs to Xu (serial) and SUBLEQ (frame). The only specification outside it is prose, and turning prose into a program is the LM's job, which the 350M does at 0.68 for a single application. That is where the 0.000 lives. It is a reading problem, not a depth problem.

One correction to the evidence base itself: "nobody has measured fast weights on serial depth" is stale. Irie et al. (NeurIPS 2021) evaluated fast weight programmers on code execution and sequential ListOps, and the sleep paper (2605.26099) measured a GDN hybrid on Rule 110 at t=32 at about 10%. Both negative.

## 4. What the evidence base permits

Three things. None is an architecture paper.

First, a universal frame executor with dynamic addressing, measured on fidelity and wall-clock. SUBLEQ shows the frame version works at 4.9M with addresses written in the program and a fixed ISA. Nobody has trained an executor to emit (address, value) writes from allocator-compiled traces of a language with variables and calls, held 0.9999 per step to thousands of steps, and reported wall-clock against a serial token trace with both under CUDA graphs. That is DRM's surviving component, and it is what would make the H200 profile (34.8% graphed, static shapes) pay. Engineering with a real payoff.

Second, the reading number: single-transition accuracy on held-out systems as a function of specification form and model size. Teacher-forced, under $10 per run. Every downstream claim depends on it and nobody has it above 0.985 for anything but a fixed language.

Third, the one open scientific cell: discreteness versus state coverage. Bansal's continuous carry with recall and truncated detached training extrapolates 600x; Altabaa's discrete beats continuous under per-step supervision with confounded training. A matched arm on held-out rules would say whether the token/latent tension is about discreteness at all. It is a measurement, and its outcome is the only one in this set that cannot be predicted from print.

The single regime where a learned executor would beat a symbolic interpreter is partial specification: rules the LM half understands, steps that need world knowledge to fill in. The evidence base is silent on it and I will not manufacture it.

## 5. Top two, written up

### Frame Machine (SFL + Snap-Tape + DRM)

Mechanism. Context is a program prefix P of at most 512 tokens, causal, KV cached once per request, followed by a frame F_t in {0..V-1}^W with W in {32, 64} and V in {64, 256}, plus one control slot for phase and halt. Input embedding at slot m is E[F_t[m]] + S_m, where E is the token embedding shared with value literals in P and S_m is a learned absolute slot vector tied to the pointer vocabulary (RoPE does not make "attend to slot 17" a one-head job; absolute slot vectors do). The block is L in {6, 12} layers, d in {256, 512}, bidirectional over the W+1 frame positions, cross-attending to P's cache; 10 to 60M parameters. Two output variants. C: logits Z in R^{(W+1) x V} through the LM head, F_{t+1}[m] = argmax_V Z[m]. D: H=2 write heads, each emitting address logits over W+1 (index W is NOP) and value logits over V; scatter argmax value into argmax address; every unaddressed slot is copied bit-exact, which is SFL's hold and DRM's NOP as one rule. The hidden state is discarded; no KV is kept for the frame.

Loss. Variant C: per-slot CE on all W slots against F*_{t+1}, weighted 100x where F*_{t+1} differs from F*_t (SUBLEQ's recipe; the content head must be trained on held slots or a gate false positive writes garbage). Variant D: CE(address) + CE(value) per head against a canonical linear-scan allocator's writes. Teacher forcing from F*_t; every (P, F*_t, F*_{t+1}) is an i.i.d. sample; no BPTT. Corruption with p=0.1: replace one slot of F*_t with a random symbol and recompute the target by re-running the reference on the corrupted frame. The target is never the gold next frame of an uncorrupted input, because that trains the model to ignore its operands. Inference: T sweeps of one CUDA graph at static shape N_P + W + 1; a halt flag freezes the frame and NOP writes fill the tail.

Numbers that generate it. Beyond Memorization 0.95/0.40/<0.25 with token CoT above 0.90 at k<=4 at the same size. The readout blind spot (2606.24898): per-loop CE leaves carried norms in the thousands at 44M and 129M. Ouro degrading past trained depth. Xu 59.5M perfect at 7,552 lines. SUBLEQ 4.9M at 99.8% to 91 steps. The H200 profile: 10% eager, 34.8% graphed, shapes must be static. The frozen receiver at 0.268 versus a rank-8 adapter at 0.994: a residual stream is not a transferable state format; integers are. The 350M at 0.000 on multi-step composition under six RL objectives: no per-step target existed.

New versus the nearest prior art (Altabaa, DNAR, SUBLEQ, 2608.31067). Three things only. Learned addressing over a bounded overwritable file from allocator-compiled traces of a language with variables and calls (SUBLEQ carries its addresses in the program; the Symbolic Neural CPU uses a fixed ALU bank). The bit-exact hold at horizons where all-slot re-prediction compounds: at 0.9995 per untouched slot, 29 untouched of 32 over 256 steps gives 0.024 exact-frame. A wall-clock number under CUDA graphs against a serial trace. Everything else is in print.

Killing experiment, in the fixed-language regime, the only regime where a >= 0.9999 is known reachable. Language: the compose task extended to named registers plus a MicroPy subset with loops and calls. A linear-scan allocator over R=64 registers emits canonical writes. Train on random programs with traces capped at 128 steps; test on held-out programs, including hand-written binary multiplication and a DPLL loop, at 1,000 to 8,000 steps. Arms at matched 20M then 60M: C, D, E (serial token trace in Xu's format, same programs, same parameters), C' (continuous control: ground-truth embedding plus N(0, sigma^2) fed back, per-step CE, sigma swept), and C'' (Bansal-style: continuous frame, recall of P, truncated detached BPTT on its own states). Day one, before any closed loop exists: per-step write accuracy under teacher forcing on at least 10^5 held-out transitions. Falsifiers. (1) D below 0.9999 per step on held-out programs at 60M: the depth claim dies on day one (0.9999^7552 = 0.47; thousands of steps need about 0.99999). (2) D minus C below 0.05 program-level exact accuracy at T=1,000 on traces where at least 90% of slots are untouched per step, on every seed: the hold is decoration. (3) Wall-clock per program for D not below 0.2x of E with both graphed on the same GPU: the efficiency claim, which is the entire point, dies. (4) C' or C'' within 0.03 of D at T=1,000: discreteness is not the variable and the tension settles the other way. Pre-registered gate: E must reproduce Xu above 95% at T >= 1,000 or the comparison is void.

Cost. $300 to $600 of GPU on one L40S or H200. Two to three weeks of one person, most of it the allocator, the bidirectional block (the Coconut backbone in src/model.py hard-codes a causal mask and max_len 512) and the closed-loop harness. The 350M checkpoint is not used.

### WRM (Frame Machine plus parallel-outermost scheduling)

Mechanism. Same block. The frame is S <= 512 heap cells times c=4 fields [opcode, arg1 pointer, arg2 pointer, value-or-status], 2,048 slots; pointers are cell indices with absolute cell embeddings tied to the pointer vocabulary so "read cell 17" is one head. The reference interpreter is modified to Gross-Knuth semantics: at each step, fire every redex whose operands are values, producing H_0 to H_D with D the critical-path depth of the dataflow DAG. The target for a cell with stale operands is "unchanged". Corruption replaces 10 to 40% of cells with H_{j-1}, H_{j-2} or blank. A two- or three-sweep unroll with hard argmax re-embedding lets the model see its own mixed canvases. The loss is weighted per changed cell, because on a deep-narrow program one cell in 2,048 changes per sweep and an unweighted loss is a copy objective. Halt on H_{j+1} == H_j with a cap, computed as a device-side equality reduction. A cell reclamation scheme (who frees cells, whether pointers move) must be written before training; PENCIL-style erasure is a name, not a scheme.

Numbers that generate it. The ones above, plus Saunshi's 1 layer x 12 loops at 99.6% on 32-operand addition because addition is log-depth; the ACL 2026 measurement that eight MDLMs to 100B do not realize their theoretical parallelism (none was trained as a closure operator); Cohere's 39 versus 62% of speed-of-light at batch 1; Xu's 2,709-line average and 7,552-line maximum at roughly ten tokens per line.

New versus 2510.14095 and 2608.31067. Program in context rather than ops in weights; a pointer-addressed heap with allocation; the D/T and wall-clock numbers on loop-carried programs. The closure-operator target with stale corruption and the depth-1 parallel reduction with halting both exist.

Killing experiment. Stage 0 on CPU for $0: instrument the reference interpreters and a MicroPy reducer with parallel-outermost semantics and measure D/T over the mathgen distribution, the invented systems, and Xu's held-out programs. Kill if median D/T is above 0.5. Two warnings: the compose task is D = T by its own docstring, and shift-and-add multiplication and DPLL are loop-carried, so Xu's own showcase programs sit near or above the line. Stage 2 is the Frame Machine experiment with frontier targets, plus: sweeps per program at most 2D, else the model fires one redex per sweep (the serial-frontier degeneration reported in 2607.20594); the frontier-versus-uniform masking ablation, where uniform reaching the same sweep count means the closure claim is false and this is an MDM on traces; accuracy within 3 points of E at matched T, reported as a curve against D so compounding is visible; wall-clock below 0.2x of E. Stage 3 (Anderson or quasi-Newton acceleration) is dropped: the per-pass k ceiling (ARMT to k=2, ACT about +1) bounds any fixed-point acceleration on chains near 2 to 3x, and argmax removes the smoothness DEER needs.

Cost. $800 to $1,200 as the generator estimated; the GPU arithmetic holds (a 60M model on 500M canvas tokens is about 2e17 FLOPs, 15 to 30 H200 minutes per run). Engineering is the cost: the reducer, the canvas codec, the allocation scheme, and a faithful Xu replication as arm E.

## 6. The first experiment: one L40S, one week

Measure the reading number, with the day-one fidelity measurement folded in.

Setup. W=32 slots, V=64 to 256, the invented formal systems with the reference implementation emitting the full frame after each step, plus radius-2 1dCA with disjoint rules. Three specification forms in the prefix: the rule as a lookup table (1dCA) or as code (the reference rule set, 100 to 300 tokens); the same rules as the generator's prose textbook; and no prefix with rules in weights, which is the fixed-language ceiling. Models: a 20M and a 60M frame block from scratch, and the 350M checkpoint with a frame head on the slot positions through the Coconut thought_hook seam. Train on single teacher-forced transitions; test on held-out systems with at least 10^5 transitions per cell so 0.9999 is resolvable; three seeds. Report per-transition exact-frame accuracy, per-slot accuracy, and one 1,000-step closed-loop curve for the best cell.

Cost. Under $50 of GPU (a 20M block on 350 positions at batch 64 is about 0.05 s per step on an L40S). The week goes to the per-step frame emitter for the generators and the frame encoder.

Falsifiers. If code-in-context reaches 0.999 at 60M and prose stays below 0.99 at 350M, the execution-cortex-for-prose claim is dead; the honest system is LM writes code, a frame or symbolic interpreter runs it, and the LM-side tool is NLI-style search against the generator's worked examples (the one thing that took NLI from 0.00 to 0.99 OOD). If code-in-context itself stays below 0.999 at 60M, the in-context frame executor is not ready and the depth sweep should not be paid for. If prose reaches 0.999 at 60M or 350M, that is the first number above 0.68 for prose rules anywhere in the evidence base, and it is the one result in this whole set that would change what anyone believes, because the reading, not the carry, is what nobody has shown.

Why this first: every judge's surviving list for SFL and Airlock named it, it costs the least, its result cannot be predicted from print, and it decides whether any of the depth work has a target.
